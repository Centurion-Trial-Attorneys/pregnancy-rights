<div class="tldr" markdown="1">
**The short version:** California pays most new parents through two state programs. State Disability Insurance (SDI) replaces part of your wages while you're disabled by pregnancy and childbirth, typically four weeks before your due date and six to eight weeks after. Paid Family Leave (PFL) then pays up to eight more weeks to bond with your baby. In 2026, both pay roughly 70% to 90% of your wages, up to $1,765 a week.
</div>

Job-protected leave and pay are two different things in California. Pregnancy disability leave (PDL) and CFRA bonding leave protect your job. SDI and PFL, both run by the Employment Development Department (EDD), put money in your account while you're out. You usually need both, and you have to apply for the pay yourself.

## Who pays for SDI and PFL?

You do, through your paycheck. Look for "CASDI" on your pay stub. The 2026 withholding rate is 1.3%, and since January 1, 2024 there's no cap on the wages it applies to, according to the [EDD's rates page](https://edd.ca.gov/en/payroll_taxes/rates_and_withholding).

To qualify, you need at least $300 in wages subject to SDI withholding during your "base period," which covers wages earned 5 to 18 months before your claim starts ([EDD DI eligibility FAQ](https://edd.ca.gov/en/Disability/FAQ_DI_Eligibility)). Eligibility doesn't depend on how long you've worked for your employer or how many employees it has, and citizenship and immigration status don't affect it, according to the [EDD's new and expecting mother overview](https://edd.ca.gov/siteassets/files/disability/pdf/pfl-newexpecting-mother-overview-englaish-09-26.pdf).

Not everyone pays in. Many government and school district employees are covered only if their employer or bargaining unit has opted into SDI. If you're self-employed, you can buy coverage through the EDD's elective program. Check your pay stubs before you count on benefits.

## How long does SDI pay during pregnancy?

The EDD's [pregnancy leave page](https://edd.ca.gov/en/disability/pregnancy-leave/) says disability benefits typically start four weeks before your due date and continue six weeks after a vaginal birth or eight weeks after a cesarean. If you have complications, such as preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, or postpartum depression, your provider can certify a longer claim or an earlier start. Disability Insurance can pay up to 52 weeks in total ([EDD benefit amounts page](https://edd.ca.gov/Disability/Calculating_DI_Benefit_Payment_Amounts.htm)).

There's a catch at the start. The first seven calendar days of a disability claim are an unpaid waiting period, so payments begin on day eight ([EDD DI claim process](https://edd.ca.gov/en/Disability/DI_Claim_Process)).

## How does Paid Family Leave work after the birth?

PFL pays up to eight weeks of benefits to a parent taking time off to bond with a new child. You have to use it within the first year after the birth, and you can break the eight weeks into pieces instead of taking them all at once ([EDD PFL benefits FAQ](https://edd.ca.gov/en/disability/FAQ_PFL_Benefits_Payments)). PFL has no waiting period. Payment begins the first day of leave.

If you gave birth and had a pregnancy disability claim, the switch is mostly automatic. After your final SDI payment, the EDD sends you the PFL bonding claim form (DE 2501FP) online or by mail. You won't have to prove your relationship to the baby, and your weekly benefit stays the same as it was on your disability claim, according to the EDD's new and expecting mother overview. You can't collect SDI and PFL for the same days.

## How much will I get each week?

For claims that start in 2025 or later, benefits are roughly 70% to 90% of your wages, depending on income. Lower-wage workers get the higher percentage. That change came from Senate Bill 951 and took effect January 1, 2025, according to the [EDD's announcement](https://www.edd.ca.gov/en/about_edd/news_releases_and_announcements/california-boosts-paid-family-leave-and-disability-benefits-to-record-levels-for-new-claims-filed-in-2025).

The EDD bases your weekly amount on your highest-earning quarter in the base period. For 2026, the minimum weekly benefit is $50 and the maximum is $1,765 ([EDD 2026 maximum](https://edd.ca.gov/siteassets/files/about_edd/quick-stats/max-weekly-benefit-amount-2026.pdf)).
## When do I have to file?

Deadlines are short, and missing one can cost you money:

- **Disability (SDI):** file no earlier than nine days after your disability begins and no later than 49 days after. Your provider's medical certification is due within the same 49 days ([EDD DI claim process](https://edd.ca.gov/en/Disability/DI_Claim_Process)).
- **Paid Family Leave:** file no earlier than the first day of your bonding leave and no later than 41 days after it starts ([EDD PFL benefits FAQ](https://edd.ca.gov/en/disability/FAQ_PFL_Benefits_Payments)).

The EDD recommends filing online through SDI Online, which you reach through a myEDD account. Most payments go out within about two weeks of a complete application.

## Do SDI and PFL protect my job?

No. The EDD says plainly that PFL "does not protect your job." The programs pay benefits. Your right to come back comes from other laws.

For birth parents, that's pregnancy disability leave (up to four months at employers with five or more employees) followed by CFRA bonding leave (up to 12 weeks if you've worked there at least a year and 1,250 hours in the past year), according to the [Civil Rights Department](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/employment/pdl-bonding-guide/). The two leaves are separate, and stacked they can reach the working days in 29⅓ workweeks (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11046(a), (d)). Our guide to [how PDL and CFRA bonding leave stack](/articles/pregnancy-disability-leave-vs-cfra-bonding-leave/) walks through the timing.

If you don't qualify for job-protected leave, you may still qualify for SDI and PFL. The reverse is also true. Check both.

## Can my employer make me use vacation or sick time?

It depends on which leave you're on.

- **During pregnancy disability leave:** you can choose to use vacation or other paid time off, but your employer can't force you to (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11044(b)(2)). Your employer can require you to use accrued sick leave during the unpaid part of PDL (§ 11044(b)(1)). The Civil Rights Department's guide says that doesn't apply while you're receiving SDI.
- **During CFRA bonding leave:** if you're receiving PFL to bond with your baby, your employer can't require you to use vacation, sick leave, or other paid time off (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11092(b)). You can agree with your employer to use paid leave to top up your PFL benefit.

Your employer also has to keep paying for your group health coverage during PDL, for up to four months, and during CFRA leave, for up to 12 workweeks (§§ 11044(c), 11092(c)).

## What if I lose my job before the baby comes?

You may still be covered. For disability benefits, you have to be working or looking for work when your disability begins. For PFL, the EDD says you need to be collecting unemployment or actively looking for work. You can't collect unemployment at the same time as SDI or PFL. If you think you were let go because you're pregnant, read our guide on [what to do in the first week after being fired while pregnant](/articles/fired-while-pregnant-first-week/).

## The practical takeaway

Before your leave, check your pay stub for CASDI, set up your myEDD account, and put the 49-day and 41-day deadlines on your calendar. Ask your provider to submit the medical certification promptly. Then give your employer written notice of your PDL and CFRA dates, since the EDD pays you but only your employer can hold your job.
