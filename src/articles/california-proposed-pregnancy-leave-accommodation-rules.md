California's Civil Rights Council has proposed the first major update in more than a decade to the state's rules on pregnancy leave, accommodations, and temporary transfers. The draft, released October 19, 2026, would say outright that telework can be a reasonable accommodation, name postpartum depression and low milk supply as covered conditions, and loosen how bonding leave can be scheduled, [according to the Civil Rights Department](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/2026/10/19/civil-rights-council-releases-proposed-regulations-to-modernize-pregnancy-leave-protections/) and reporting by [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/california-proposal-post-partum-depression-low-milk-supply-pregnancy-protections/833593/) and [Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/2540155/calif-eyes-updating-pregnancy-leave-workplace-protections-).

These are proposed regulations. They are not in effect, and the public can weigh in before anything is final.

## What would the proposed rules change?

According to the Civil Rights Department's [announcement](https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/950799817/civil-rights-council-releases-proposed-regulations-to-modernize-pregnancy-leave-protections), the proposal would:

- **Name more covered conditions.** The rules would clarify that pregnancy protections extend to related medical conditions such as postpartum depression, low milk supply, and lower back pain.
- **Add accommodation examples.** Telework, changes to the work environment, and temporarily pausing some essential job functions would be listed as possible reasonable accommodations.
- **Expand who can support a request.** Nurses, doulas, and psychiatrists would be added to the list of providers who can support an employee's need for an accommodation.
- **Loosen bonding leave scheduling.** The proposal would remove the requirement that baby-bonding leave be taken in two-week increments.

The department said the update is meant to reflect recent changes to California law and, where appropriate, line state rules up with the federal Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA) and its regulations, while going further than federal law in some areas.

## Who is behind the proposal, and what did they say?

The Civil Rights Council is the body within California's Civil Rights Department that writes regulations explaining how the state's civil rights laws apply. "As the workplace continues to change, our state's civil rights protections have to keep pace," Civil Rights Department Director Kevin Kish said in a statement [quoted by HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/california-proposal-post-partum-depression-low-milk-supply-pregnancy-protections/833593/). He added that the updates "will help ensure that California law not only meets but exceeds protections at the federal level."

Coverage we reviewed did not include a response from employer groups. Proposals like this often draw comments from businesses during the rulemaking process, and the final rules can differ from the draft.

## How do the current rules work?

Today, California's regulations already list modified duties, schedule changes, more frequent breaks, seating, and lactation breaks as examples of reasonable accommodations (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11035(s)). Telework isn't on that list, though the list isn't exhaustive.

The current definition of "health care provider" includes physicians, nurse practitioners, nurse midwives, licensed midwives, clinical psychologists, clinical social workers, and others who treat the pregnancy-related condition (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11035(n)).

For bonding leave under the California Family Rights Act (CFRA), the current rule sets a basic minimum of two weeks per block, though employers must grant shorter blocks on at least two occasions (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11090(d)). The proposal would remove that two-week requirement.

## When could the changes take effect?

Not yet. Written comments are due by 5 p.m. Pacific time on December 8, 2026, and the council will hold a public hearing at 11 a.m. Pacific time that same day, according to the department and [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/california-proposal-post-partum-depression-low-milk-supply-pregnancy-protections/833593/). After that, the council can revise the draft before adopting final rules. Details on how to submit comments are in the council's notice of proposed rulemaking on the [Civil Rights Council page](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/civilrightscouncil/).

## What this means for California workers

For now, nothing changes about your rights. The current rules still apply, and they're already strong. If your California employer has five or more employees, it can't refuse a reasonable accommodation your health care provider says is medically advisable because of pregnancy, childbirth, or a related condition (Gov. Code § 12945(a)(3)(A)). If it has 15 or more, the federal PWFA also requires reasonable accommodations for known pregnancy-related limitations unless they would cause undue hardship (42 U.S.C. § 2000gg-1(1)).

That means you can ask for telework today. The current list of accommodations is a set of examples, and the question is whether the change you need is reasonable for your job. If remote work would let you keep doing your job while managing pregnancy symptoms or postpartum recovery, ask for it in writing and include your provider's note. Our guide to [pregnancy rights at work in California](/articles/pregnancy-rights-at-work-california/) explains the basics, and our guide to [lactation breaks](/articles/lactation-breaks-california/) covers pumping rights.

If the proposal is adopted, it would make some of these requests easier to make and harder to refuse. If you want a say, the comment period runs through December 8.
