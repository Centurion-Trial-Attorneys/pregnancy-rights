Your rights, from the first trimester to the first year

California and federal law protect you at every stage. These are the rights employers get wrong most often.

  • No discrimination

    Your employer can't fire you, demote you, cut your hours, or pass you over because you're pregnant or just had a baby.

    Pregnancy rights at work

  • Reasonable accommodations

    More breaks, a seat, lighter duties, or a temporary transfer when your health care provider advises it.

    Accommodation questions

  • Job-protected leave

    Up to four months of pregnancy disability leave, then up to 12 weeks of bonding leave if you qualify, with your job waiting.

    How leave works

  • Pumping breaks and a private space

    Break time to pump whenever you need it, and a private place to do it that is never a bathroom.

    Lactation breaks

  • Leave after a loss

    Up to five days off after a miscarriage, stillbirth, failed adoption, failed surrogacy, or unsuccessful assisted reproduction.

    Reproductive loss leave

  • No retaliation

    Asking for an accommodation, taking leave, or complaining can't cost you your job. Punishment for it is illegal.

    Retaliation questions

The numbers California employers should know

Most of these rights apply once your employer has five employees.

  • 4

    Months of pregnancy leave

    Up to four months of job-protected leave while you're disabled by pregnancy or childbirth, with health coverage continued.

    Gov. Code § 12945(a)(1), (a)(2)(A)
  • 12

    Weeks of bonding leave

    Up to 12 workweeks to bond with a new child if you've worked there more than a year and 1,250 hours.

    Gov. Code § 12945.2(a)
  • 5

    Days after a loss

    Up to five days of leave after a miscarriage, stillbirth, or other reproductive loss.

    Gov. Code § 12945.6(b)(1)
  • $100

    A day for denied pumping time

    The Labor Commissioner can fine an employer for each day it denies break time or a proper space to pump.

    Lab. Code § 1033(c)
