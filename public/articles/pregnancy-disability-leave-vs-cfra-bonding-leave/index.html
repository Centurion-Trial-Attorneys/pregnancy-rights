How Do Pregnancy Disability Leave and CFRA Bonding Leave Stack in California?

California gives you up to four months of pregnancy disability leave, then up to 12 more weeks of bonding leave. Here's how the two fit together.

Guide · By Ryan Stygar, Employment Attorney · November 2, 2026

The short version: In California, pregnancy disability leave (PDL) and baby-bonding leave under the California Family Rights Act (CFRA) are two separate rights. PDL covers the time you're disabled by pregnancy or childbirth, up to four months. CFRA bonding leave is up to 12 more workweeks to care for your baby, and your employer can't count your PDL against it. Put together, a full-time worker who qualifies for both can be away for roughly seven months with the job protected.

Most people picture "maternity leave" as one block of time. California splits it into two pieces with different rules. Knowing where one ends and the other starts helps you plan and makes it harder for an employer to shortchange you.

What is pregnancy disability leave?

PDL is time off while you're disabled by pregnancy, childbirth, or a related medical condition. You can take up to four months (Gov. Code § 12945(a)(1)). It applies at employers with five or more full-time or part-time employees (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11035(h)).

"Four months" means the days you would normally work in four calendar months. For someone working full time, that comes to about 17⅓ weeks (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11035(l)).

PDL isn't only for the weeks after delivery. "Disabled" includes severe morning sickness, prenatal care, bed rest your provider orders, gestational diabetes, and recovery from childbirth (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11035(f)). You don't have to take it all at once. Your health care provider decides how long you're disabled, so PDL lasts as long as that disability does, up to the four-month cap.

There's no minimum time on the job for PDL. If you started last month and your employer has five or more employees, you can still take it.

What is CFRA bonding leave?

CFRA gives eligible employees up to 12 workweeks of job-protected leave in a 12-month period to bond with a new child (Gov. Code § 12945.2(a), (b)(5)(A)). It covers employers with five or more employees (Gov. Code § 12945.2(b)(4)(A)).

Unlike PDL, CFRA has eligibility rules. You need more than 12 months of service with your employer and at least 1,250 hours worked in the 12 months before your leave starts (Gov. Code § 12945.2(a)). Bonding leave must be taken within one year of the birth.

Bonding leave isn't tied to a medical condition. You don't need a doctor to say you're disabled. You're taking it because you have a new baby. That's also why a partner or other parent can take CFRA bonding leave, even though only the birthing parent takes PDL.

Can my employer count PDL against my CFRA leave?

No. The two are separate. You can take up to four months of PDL and then up to 12 workweeks of CFRA bonding leave, for a combined total of four months plus 12 workweeks (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11093(a), (d)).

If an employer tells you your "maternity leave" is 12 weeks total, or that your time out before the birth used up your bonding leave, push back in writing. Those are the two ways the stacking rule most often gets ignored.

What does a stacked leave look like in practice?

Here are two examples for a full-time worker who qualifies for both:

  • Typical recovery. Your provider takes you off work two weeks before your due date and clears you to return eight weeks after delivery. That's 10 weeks of PDL. Your 12 weeks of CFRA bonding leave can start the day PDL ends, so you're out about 22 weeks.
  • Complications. Your provider puts you on leave early and keeps you out through the full four months of PDL, about 17⅓ weeks. Your 12 weeks of bonding leave come after that, for about 29⅓ weeks, or roughly seven months.

Treat these as illustrations. Your PDL depends on what your provider certifies, and your actual dates may differ.

Do I have to take bonding leave all at once?

No, but there are limits. Under the current rule, your employer can generally require bonding leave in blocks of at least two weeks, though it has to grant shorter blocks on at least two occasions (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11090(d)). California's Civil Rights Council has proposed removing the two-week requirement, but that change isn't in effect. Our write-up on the proposed pregnancy leave rules explains where that stands.

What if I don't qualify for CFRA yet?

You still get PDL. If you haven't hit 12 months or 1,250 hours, the four months of pregnancy disability leave are still yours, because PDL has no service requirement. What you may lose is the guaranteed 12 weeks of bonding leave afterward.

Check your employer's handbook anyway, since some employers offer more than the law requires.

Is my job protected during both leaves?

Yes. When PDL ends, you have a right to return to the same position, and your employer must put that guarantee in writing if you ask (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11043(a)). CFRA leave is also job-protected (Gov. Code § 12945.2(a)).

Your employer must also keep paying for your group health coverage during PDL, at the same level as if you were working, for up to four months (Gov. Code § 12945(a)(2)(A); Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11044(c)).

Using these rights is protected too. It's illegal for an employer to interfere with or deny your pregnancy leave rights (Gov. Code § 12945(a)(4)). For CFRA, California's regulations say your employer can't discourage you from taking leave you're entitled to, and "cannot use the taking of CFRA leave as a negative factor in employment actions" (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 11094(a), (b)).

Will I get paid during PDL or CFRA leave?

Neither PDL nor CFRA requires your employer to pay you, though many workers use sick time or vacation, and some employers offer paid leave. Pay usually comes from the state. State Disability Insurance may replace part of your wages while you're disabled by pregnancy, and Paid Family Leave pays up to eight weeks of benefits for bonding with a new child, according to the Employment Development Department.

PDL and CFRA protect your job. The EDD programs pay you. Qualifying for one doesn't guarantee the other.

What should I do before my leave starts?

  • Give notice in writing. Tell HR your expected PDL dates and that you plan to take CFRA bonding leave right after. Ask for written confirmation of your return date.
  • Ask for the reinstatement guarantee in writing. You're entitled to it for PDL.
  • Keep your provider's notes. Save copies of every certification you give your employer, outside of work systems.
  • Watch for changes when you come back. A new title, lost duties, or a sudden bad review after leave is worth writing down. Our guide to pregnancy rights at work in California covers retaliation, and our guide on what to do if you're fired while pregnant walks through the first steps.

Leave is something the law gives you. Plan both pieces, get the dates in writing, and don't let anyone fold them into one.
